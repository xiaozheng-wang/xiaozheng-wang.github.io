I am currently a Ph.D. student in the Extended Reality and Artificial Intelligence (Xrai) Lab at Virginia Tech, advised by Dr. [Ryan P. McMahan](https://website.cs.vt.edu/people/faculty/ryan-p-mcmahan.html).

#### Research Interests

I study human motion modeling, synthesis, and reconstruction in extended reality, with an emphasis on individual differences, identity, and generalization across users and tasks. My research combines machine learning, statistical analysis, and XR systems to investigate how movement characteristics are represented and preserved in recorded and synthetic motion. My current work explores full-body reconstruction from sparse tracking and biomechanical considerations in motion modeling for virtual avatars.

**Keywords:** Human Motion Modeling and Synthesis; Sparse Tracking and Motion Reconstruction; Individual Differences and Behavioral Identity; Cross-Task Generalization; Machine Learning; XR and Virtual Avatars; Biomechanics.

#### Research Experience

**Extended Reality and Artificial Intelligence (Xrai) Lab, Virginia Tech**  
*Ph.D. Student Researcher | Advisor: Ryan P. McMahan*

- Developed a motion synthesis pipeline combining dynamic time warping and trajectory interpolation; generated and released 100 synthetic XR motion trajectories and evaluated their utility for motion-based user identification.
- Developed a pipeline for constructing persistent synthetic identities by combining individual movement patterns with participant-derived body scaling; evaluated identity recognition, real–synthetic distinguishability, and gender inference across 100 real and 100 synthetic identities.
- Applied machine learning and statistical analysis to investigate how spatial representations and motion characteristics influence identity recognition and generalization across tasks.
- Contributed to a comparative study of spatial encodings and position- versus velocity-based features for VR user identification, examining performance within and across applications.
- Contributed to the CLEVER–Job Simulator dataset, comprising VR motion and interaction data from 95 participants, and mentored three collaborating researchers.
- Currently investigating human motion modeling, synthesis, and full-body reconstruction from sparse XR tracking, using Unity and virtual avatars to explore individual movement characteristics, biomechanical considerations, and generalization across users and tasks.
